# Design Rationale and Failure Patterns

## Current references outrank prior knowledge

A collectible figure can depict a specific costume, facial state, damaged garment, accessory, or pose that differs from a character's common canonical appearance. Preserve what is visibly supported instead of substituting a familiar generic design.

## Spatial proximity is not body ownership

Busy figure photographs often contain a second statue, creature, hand, weapon, flame, or display element near the subject. Before retaining it, trace a plausible connection to the subject and verify the relationship across additional views. This prevents the highest-priority regression: importing another character's limb into the subject.

## Multiple images provide different evidence

Front views are usually strongest for eyes and face; profiles for hair volume; full-body views for clothing and proportion; close-ups for hands, jewelry, and weapon details. Weight each image by clarity and relevance rather than blending every visible detail.

## Crop selection protects anatomy

Circular compositions punish tall full-body poses. If showing the complete figure makes the person tiny or forces compressed legs and torso, use a bust or half-body crop. Preserve the identity-bearing action when possible, but never at the cost of malformed structure.

## Background motifs need evidence and hierarchy

An associated creature, eye, weapon effect, flame, or architecture can support the character when references justify it. Convert supporting motifs into background hierarchy rather than allowing them to compete with the face or create ambiguous limbs. When canon is uncertain, use non-narrative atmosphere.

## A physical mockup is an edit, not a redesign

The mockup must insert the already-approved artwork. A realistic result preserves the physical rim and places highlights, reflections, noise, exposure, curvature, and perspective over the new design. If the face, hand, costume, or background is reinterpreted, the mockup fails even when it looks photographic.

## Details that must not become universal style rules

- A particular energy color belongs to the current character, not the Skill.
- Moons, petals, rubble, creature eyes, and flames are optional evidence-based motifs.
- Gold rims and decorative borders are not default requirements.
- A pose from one example must not be imposed on another character.
- Platform text, seller handles, logos, watermarks, and display bases are source artifacts rather than character design.
